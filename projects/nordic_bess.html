What a Battery Earns in SE3,
and Which Market It Comes From

A revenue-stacking model for a 10 MW / 20 MWh battery in Swedish bidding zone SE3. The dispatch optimiser chooses, hour by hour, how much capacity to sell into FCR-D, FCR-N and day-ahead arbitrage, under the physical limits that stop a battery from promising the same MW twice. The question behind it: after the 2024 collapse in Swedish FCR-D prices, how much of a Nordic battery’s revenue is still bankable?

Background

Why SE3, and why now

Project 04 on this site derives a price series from generator cost curves and shows that day-ahead arbitrage alone earns a battery nothing below a 1.181 price ratio. That page ends with a caveat: in the Nordic system, reserve revenue often exceeds arbitrage revenue. This project takes that caveat seriously and models the reserves.

Sweden is the right place to do it. Svenska kraftnät procures frequency containment reserves (FCR-N, FCR-D up and FCR-D down) in hourly capacity auctions, and batteries entered that market faster than demand grew. Prequalified FCR-D capacity went from under 10 MW in early 2023 to roughly 600 MW by late 2024, against procurement needs below 550 MW. Marginal pricing replaced pay-as-bid from 1 February 2024. Prices that had sat in the tens of EUR per MW and hour fell to single digits within a year. A revenue model calibrated on 2023 FCR-D prices overstates a Nordic battery’s income badly, and the point of this project is to quantify by how much.

The question: for a 10 MW / 20 MWh battery in SE3, how much can it earn from FCR-D, FCR-N and day-ahead arbitrage together, how does that split change between 2023 and 2025, and what does the shift mean for a lender sizing debt against it?
Method

One optimiser, three products

Each hour the model chooses three things: a day-ahead dispatch (charge or discharge, in MW), an FCR-D reservation and an FCR-N reservation. The three compete for the same two resources:

  • Rated power. Reserved MW plus dispatched MW cannot exceed the inverter rating. A MW sold as FCR-D is a MW that cannot be used to discharge into a high day-ahead hour.
  • State of charge. Reserved capacity must be deliverable in both directions for a full activation, so the battery can be neither empty nor full while it holds reserves. Each reserved MW pins a slice of the energy store on both sides.

The schedule that maximises total revenue over the day is found by exact dynamic programming over a discretised state-of-charge grid. Prices for the day are assumed known, so the result is an upper bound on what a real forecast-driven strategy could capture, and the gap between the two is one of the quantities the project is designed to measure. The optimiser is written in plain NumPy with no solver dependency; a linear-programming version is planned and the test suite will check that both agree.

The code is structured as a package rather than a notebook: an asset class, a price loader with a documented CSV schema, the optimiser, a day-by-day runner that carries state of charge between days, and a command-line entry point. That was a deliberate choice after a conversation with an optimisation engineer at a battery trading firm, whose point was that the formulation is the easy part and the integration is where the work is.

Three structural changes fall inside the 2023 to 2025 window and have to be handled explicitly rather than averaged over: the move from pay-as-bid averages to marginal prices in February 2024, the switch of the day-ahead market to 15-minute products in October 2025, and the migration of FCR procurement to the Nordic MMS platform in April 2026. The data pipeline keeps a regime flag for each so results can be reported per regime.

What comes next

Planned results

  1. Build the merged hourly SE3 dataset for 2023 to 2025 and recompute the annual FCR averages from the hourly data, since no official annual figures exist.
  2. Run the optimiser over each year and report revenue per MW-year by product, so the FCR-D compression shows up as a shift in the revenue mix rather than a single headline number.
  3. Measure the foresight premium by replacing perfect knowledge of day-ahead prices with a simple forecast.
  4. Add a project-finance layer: debt sizing against the contracted-looking part of the revenue stack, DSCR under each year’s prices, and equity IRR. This is the piece that turns a dispatch model into an answer about bankability.
  5. Cross-check revenue per MW-year against a published battery benchmark for a market with comparable data coverage.
Honesty

Limitations, as of v0.1

No real-data results are published yet. The optimiser has only been run on synthetic prices to verify that it behaves correctly. No revenue figure from those runs appears on this page, because none of them means anything.
  • Perfect foresight within each day. Results will be an upper bound until the forecast step is in.
  • Symmetric reserves. FCR-D up and down are modelled with the same state-of-charge requirement; the real products are separately priced and separately procured, and the data pipeline keeps them separate.
  • No degradation cost, grid charges or intraday trading.
  • Terminal state of charge is valued at zero each day, which slightly discourages ending the day charged.
